Med-Shicheng Evaluation Standards Construction
Source: Med-Shicheng supplemental materials, Appendix B.1
============================================================

Due to the continuous advancement of large language models (LLMs), their ability to accomplish various complex tasks composed of multiple individual tasks is also constantly improving. Various medical LLMs built upon such LLMs have begun to output results that are complex in content and logically sound. Currently, the lack of systematic evaluation standards for medical LLMs inevitably leads to the omission of important dimensions when evaluating such complex outputs—whether using general-purpose LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek, GPT-5) as judges or involving human doctors/medical experts. Therefore, to establish a systematic, comprehensive, and objective evaluation of the increasingly complex outputs of medical LLMs, this study focuses on complex task scenarios of TCM (Traditional Chinese Medicine) and adopts the Delphi method to develop standards for medical LLMs to inherit the distinguished TCM physicians’ specialized knowledge systems. The Delphi method is used to develop expert consensus and is widely applied in health science research, where it is commonly employed to determine priorities and establish clinical guidelines [1].

Through a review of literature in the fields of distinguished TCM physician inheritance and innovation as well as artificial intelligence, and interviews with TCM experts and AI researchers, the researchers identified five main dimensions for model evaluation, sub-dimensions under the main dimensions, and evaluators from the TCM expert pool, totaling 19 items. These items were subsequently quantified and graded based on their importance, with scores ranging from 1 to 5, where higher scores indicate greater importance. An online questionnaire was distributed for this study. Two rounds of questionnaires were ultimately completed. In the second round, the results of the first round were presented to the experts, who were then asked to complete the second round of the questionnaire based on the initial results. A consensus was formed according to the outcomes of the second round.

Eleven participants completed the questionnaires, with the same group of individuals participating in both rounds. Among them, six held intermediate professional titles, three held associate senior titles, and two held full senior titles. Seven held doctoral degrees, three held master's degrees, and one held a bachelor's degree. All of them were clinical practitioners of TCM, with some having become academic authoritative figures in the field, ensuring a high level of authority. For the main dimensions, items with an average score ≥4 and a coefficient of variation ≤0.2 across both questionnaire rounds were selected as approved criteria. The evaluation assigns ratings to each of the five main dimensions, with different weights set according to their importance—higher weights indicate greater significance. Among these, the "Dimension of Similarity to distinguished Physicians" was established as the primary evaluation criterion for task completion and assigned the highest weight. The "Dimension of Consistency with TCM Philosophy", serving as the key measure for assessing the model’s performance in fundamental TCM tasks, was assigned the second-highest weight. Dimensions such as "Safety", "Content Completeness", and "Fluency" regarded as secondary characteristics of task completion, were assigned moderate weights.

In terms of sub-dimensions, "Similarity in Diagnostic and Treatment Characteristics and Medical Philosophy" was included to fully reflect the individualized features of TCM experts. Sub-dimensions such as "Syndrome Differentiation Process", "Treatment and Medication", and "Thinking Process" were highly emphasized. To precisely explore the accuracy of generated tasks in depth, we aligned evaluation items more closely with task outputs: the diagnosis and treatment process of distinguished physicians corresponds to the TCM etiology and pathogenesis analysis, the syndrome differentiation process corresponds to syndrome differentiation results, and the similarity in treatment and medication habits corresponds to treatment principles, and TCM prescription, respectively. The item on similarity in diagnostic and treatment characteristics and medical philosophy corresponds to the doctor’s the distinguished theory application.

The study adopted a human-AI collaborative approach for evaluation. Based on current automated evaluation capabilities and previous research [2], sub-dimensions under the "Dimension of Similarity to distinguished Physicians" were evaluated automatically, while human physicians assessed all main dimensions. The human evaluators consisted of either the famous physicians themselves or their mentees. The final dimensions and weight items included in the evaluation are presented in the table 3.
